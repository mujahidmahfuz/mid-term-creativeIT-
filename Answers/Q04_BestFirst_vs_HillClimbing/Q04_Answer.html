Q4. Best-First vs Hill Climbing: Expanding the Search Space and Action Space

Question. What do best-first search and hill climbing have in common? How can best-first search expand the search space? Can the action space and the search space grow together? Using Diagram 3, can we control the convergence speed, and can it differ at A, B and C? For Q3, define two extra operations that expand the search space and describe their effect on the goal state.

1. What they have in common

  • Both are informed (heuristic) searches. They use an evaluation score to decide which design to look at next.
  • Both are greedy: they pick what looks best now, not what is best after many steps.
  • Both expand a node by applying the same legal actions and scoring the children.
Hill climbingBest-first search
MemoryOnly the current designAn OPEN list of every design generated
Next nodeBest neighbour of the current oneBest design anywhere in OPEN
BacktrackingNone, so it gets stuck on a local maximumYes, it can jump back to an older, promising node
CostCheapMore memory and more designs scored

In short, hill climbing is best-first search with a memory of size one.

2. Assumptions

  • Diagram 3 is a score hill f(x) with its peak at x = 6. A is the foot (x = 1.3), B is the slope and C is the peak.
  • The airport model is the one from Q3: blueprint (10 gates, 6 counters, area 100, shops landside, waiting area 14).
  • Best-first gets a budget of 400 expansions, so the comparison is fair.

3. How can best-first search expand the search space?

Hill climbing throws away every neighbour it does not choose. Best-first keeps them all in OPEN. When the current branch stops improving, it simply pops the next most promising design, even one from several steps ago. So the explored space grows in many directions at once. In our run, best-first scored 875 designs with actions a–e, while hill climbing scored only 117.

function BestFirst(S0, actions, budget):
    OPEN ← {S0};  SEEN ← {S0};  best ← S0
    while OPEN not empty and expansions < budget:
        n ← design in OPEN with the highest Score
        best ← better of (best, n)
        for each action in actions:                 # the action space can be expanded here
            child ← apply(action, n)
            if child is legal and child not in SEEN: add to OPEN and SEEN
    return best

4. Can the action space and search space expand at the same time? Yes

Every new action creates new children, so a bigger action space automatically makes the reachable search space bigger. In the airport, designs reachable in 6 moves rise from 650 (actions a–e) to 1765 (actions a–g). That is the idea of "expand search space + expand action space together": a new action is a new edge, and a new edge leads to new states.

5. Controlling convergence speed (Diagram 3)

Yes, we can control it, and it can be different at A, B and C. The tool is the step size (in simulated annealing the same job is done by the temperature).

ZoneWhereRule usedWhy
AFoot, flat groundBig step (1.2)The slope is tiny, so small steps would crawl. Be bold.
BSteep slopeMedium step (0.6)Follow the gradient steadily. Fast and safe.
CNear the peakHalve the step after each overshootA big step would jump over the peak. Fine-tune.

The small step is accurate but slow. The big step is fast but misses the peak. The adaptive step gets both: it reached the peak in 12 moves, about 4 times faster than the small step.

8. Summary

Best-first and hill climbing are both greedy and heuristic. Best-first adds an OPEN list, so it can leave a local peak and explore more. Step size (or temperature) controls convergence: big at A, steady at B, shrinking at C. The biggest gain came from expanding the action space with f and g, which grew the search space from 650 to 1765 designs and moved the best score from 79.40 to 82.87.
